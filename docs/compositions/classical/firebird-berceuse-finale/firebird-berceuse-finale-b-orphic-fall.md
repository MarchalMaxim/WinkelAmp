# Firebird, Berceuse and Finale · B · "Orphic fall"

**Piece:** Stravinsky, *The Firebird* suite (1919): Berceuse and Finale. Show `firebird-berceuse-finale-b-orphic-fall`, open with `/?show=firebird-berceuse-finale-b-orphic-fall`.
**Concept:** the spell as radial grains. Nebula's dust carries the lullaby's voice and Mandala's lace carries the harp. Under both, one Tunnel runs through the whole show. It starts as a slow, pale cradle that rocks with the harp. The spell freezes it. In the hymn's crescendo it becomes a fall, and it lurches on the 7/4 stamps. Fluid fireworks go off only on the ff blows.
**Keywords:** lullaby spell, magic feather, rebirth, volcano fire.
**Palette:** violet, cyan and white dust; gold lace; pale bone rings in the lullaby; ember tunnel in the Finale; crimson smoke.

## What to judge
- **Tunnel as a long-exposure filter (new).** Tunnel redraws the whole canvas a little larger and turned on every frame, so it adds wakes to every widget above it. In the Berceuse it runs slow, with its walls fading at about half their default speed (trail 0.5). Is this a theme-maker or mush?
- **Nebula and Mandala as a pair** (untested): dust on the melody, lace on the harp, over 3 minutes of pp.
- **Tunnel and the original fluid fireworks in one show** (untested), in the 7/4 and the Maestoso.
- **Mandala in four small windows** (the harp-bridge constellation), and a Nebula squashed into a floor band (hymn only).
- **One widget as the spine of the form:** the same Tunnel is a cradle, then frozen, then gone, then a fall, then lurching, then stopped dead.

**Serendipity:**
- **The cradle (critic).** Tunnel is a "fall" widget. Here it is cast against type: slow, pale and long-exposure under a pp lullaby. Its wakes made the dust look like glowing gold feathers. That was not planned. It fits the "magic feather" keyword and the Firebird herself. The harp's rocking flips its turning, so the frame swings like a cradle. The spell freezes it into a glow, and the same widget later becomes the fall, so you watch the spell lift. It replaced the composer's crimson floor band, which read as a smear.
- **The constellation (composer):** the harp bridge is dealt out like a pendulum across four small mandala windows.
- **The hard cut (composer):** on the subito pp, all layers stop dead.
- **The tunnel stops on the F1 blow (composer):** the fall stops on the lowest note of the piece.
- **The final swell (composer):** it uses 7 restatements of the chord taken from the audio onsets of the roll. These are real sounds, but they are not in the analyst's picks.

**Knobs moved from our defaults, and why:**
- **cradle tunnel:** speed 0.4, trail 0.5, palette bone, shape rings. These make it a cradle, not a fall.
- **nebula:** amount 6500 and threads 2.2 on the lullaby; burst 1.5 on the volcano dust.
- **mandala:** life 6 on the harp; size 1.15 on the sun.
- **fireworks:** hue 0.985 and range 0.08 for crimson smoke. The composer's 0.02 read as muddy salmon.

**Known weak spots:**
- The Berceuse still leaves black at the edges. It is truer to the pp than a full screen would be.
- The dust disappears in the tutti.
- The Maestoso is black around the sun.
- In the full preview, 5:24-6:55 still shows the old salmon smoke. `renders/preview/preview-firebird-berceuse-finale-b-orphic-fall-324-362.mp4` shows the crimson one.
