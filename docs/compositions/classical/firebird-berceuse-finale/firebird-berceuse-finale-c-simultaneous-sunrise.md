# Firebird, Berceuse and Finale · C · Simultaneous sunrise

**Piece:** Stravinsky, *The Firebird*: Berceuse and Finale (6:53 shown, from the first harp strike to the last chord, before the applause).
**Concept:** One hero only. Robert Delaunay's three discs of simultaneous colour (bass, middle and treble) are the whole Firebird. The Finale is one long crescendo, so the show is one long approach: a small painting low in a black sky that rises and grows with every statement of the hymn, until we fall into it at the tutti.
**Keywords:** triumphant sunrise, rebirth, lullaby spell.
**Palette:** Delaunay's simultaneous contrasts. Every note has its colour on the circle of fifths, and its complementary colour lights up beside it. B major sits on the cool side (viridian, cerulean, violet). Warm reds only arrive with the white-key C/G harmony of the 7/4.

## What to judge

- **Delaunay as the only widget for 7 minutes** (it is rated LOVE, but this is the first time it has been used in a show). Does one painting hold the attention, carried only by its knobs (contrast, cut, turn, memory, pulse), which change note by note, plus the distance from which we see it?
- **Distance as form (new).** The berceuse is a small painting standing on the bottom edge of a black screen. It steps closer on the swell and falls back at the harp bridge. It moves further away as the music falls asleep and freezes in the spell. Then every hymn statement brings it closer (0.66, 0.8, 0.95, 1.12 of full size) until the 2x fall at the tutti. Each step is a cut on a real note. Does the approach read as a sunrise? Is the small night too spare for three minutes? (That length is the cast's own open question.)
- **The spell's dark glaze:** after the last frozen chord change, the far-off painting sinks to a third of its weight with no note, and the horn's first note wakes it.
- **Two scales in the Maestoso:** a giant bass disc lies under the composition. It now runs at presence 0.4, so its resting glaze is nearly black and only the struck bass arcs glow. Does it read as one picture with one focus, or still as a moiré?
- **The final chord:** the low sun returns. A band of B major climbs it from bass to treble over 10 s, and on the last stroke it cuts to a sun bigger than the screen.

**Serendipity:**
- The camera is the form: one widget seen from eight distances, so a 4-minute crescendo becomes a sunrise. The crop of the canvas becomes the horizon the painting stands on.
- The farthest view is the spell, not the night.
- Presence is cast against type in the Maestoso. The giant disc is black ground and only its struck arcs show.
- From the composer: the 2x fall into the painting at the tutti, the lone treble sun in the hush, and a fresh composition for the final chord, whose last stroke cuts to a sun bigger than the screen.
