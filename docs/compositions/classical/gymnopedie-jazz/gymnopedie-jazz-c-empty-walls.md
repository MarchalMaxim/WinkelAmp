# Empty walls: Satie, Gymnopédie No. 1 (jazz mix)

Show: `gymnopedie-jazz-c-empty-walls` (open as `/?show=gymnopedie-jazz-c-empty-walls`)

- **Concept:** a white gallery at night, with LeWitt as the only widget. Every wall is one LeWitt instance listening to one strand of the music. Each part of the arrangement starts a fresh wall with the next rule, and each break leaves a wall standing unfinished.
- **Keywords:** empty rooms, drifting stillness, slow dance, Sunday afternoon.
- **Palette:** a pale plaster wall, fine coloured-pencil lines (graphite, red, blue, yellow) and the rule written in pencil at the bottom left. The tune is the only colour on the theme walls; the band stays graphite.

## What to judge

- **LeWitt alone for five minutes.** Can one widget carry a whole song if every wall is a different instance, strand and rule? This combination has not been judged before.
- **Small copies as a gallery.** In the intro and interlude, several walls hang as small copies on black, one per strand, each lighting with its strand's first note. Do they read as "empty rooms", or just as too much black?
- **Rules as form.** Does each new rule (corners, sides, arcs, through, squares, centre, chain) feel like a new part of the music?
- **New: the groove as a step grid (3:27-3:55).** The drums are placed by *when* they fall, not by their GM key. The eighth of the 3/4 bar sets the column and the drum sets the row: kick at the floor, then snare, toms and ride, with crash at the top. Under the squares rule every hit hatches its own cell again, so the cells the beat insists on go dark and the off-steps stay pale. A drum's "pitch" was never a pitch, so this placement is an honest one.
- **The same rule three times in a row.** Phrase 8 of the return is squares (3:14), the cadence-2 star is centre for about 6 s, and the groove grid is squares again (3:27). Does this read as a rhyme, or as the concept stalling?
- **Stillness as an image.** In both ring-outs the crayon cadence star stands with nothing moving, for 3 s. No fake attacks.

**Serendipity:**
- (composer) Every wall is its own instance on its own strand, hung as small copies. The opening is a gallery assembling itself in the dark, and the end goes back to it.
- (composer) Per-note knob values: the tune in colour over a graphite band, the ff blows in crayon. On the solo wall a second rule ("through") starts at the harmonic lift and is laid over the chain without wiping it.
- (composer) The phrase-8 squares walls were found in the previews.
- (critic) The groove is drawn as a drum machine. Drums are placed by their eighth of the bar instead of their GM key, so the breakbeat becomes a step grid of hatched squares. It answers the busiest drumming of the piece and rhymes with the phrase-8 squares, which come back here as a rhythm.

**Known weak spots:**
- The intro keeps 69 s mostly black around small walls. This is deliberate ("empty rooms"), but it breaks the full-screen rule.
- The ticks wall crowds its right edge.
- The captions on the small walls are too small to read.
- The opaque walls crossfade through a brief grey veil (0.25-0.4 s).
- After about 3:43 the step grid mostly darkens rather than changes.
