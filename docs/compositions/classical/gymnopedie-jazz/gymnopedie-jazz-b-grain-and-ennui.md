# Gymnopédie (jazz) · B · Grain and ennui

**Piece:** Satie's Gymnopédie No. 1 as a jazz arrangement: a vamp, the band, the theme twice, then a groove and a piano solo.
**Concept:** dots and stains. Ennui is huge green stains that can't be bothered. Lazy lies under everything, hears every note, and moves for almost none of them. Over it, the comping dabs pointillist dots. Two halftone screens print the band: a coarse red one for the bass and a fine pale one for the brushes, hats and ticks. Cells puts down a pebble for each melody note, and gliss curls amber smoke on the runs and fills.
**Keywords:** ennui, soft mist, Sunday afternoon (Seurat's dots), smoky late-night.
**Palette:** moss-green stains that sour slowly towards acid; magenta and rose dusk dots; red and pale halftone screens; amber smoke.

## What to judge

- **New family, never tried together before:** lazy, pointillism, halftone (two copies), cells and gliss.
- **Lazy as the ground:** one clip from 0:14 to the end. Its green sours and its stains grow fatter over the whole piece, whatever the groove does. Does that read as ennui, or just as a green background?
- **Two halftones split by strand:** red for the bass, pale for the drums. The brushes, hats and ride were taken from the audio (5-11 kHz onsets), not from the notes.
- **Cells with two blends:** "lighten" in the theme, "clear" in the solo. In the solo, the green shows only in the gaps between the pebbles, like the grout in a terrazzo floor. Some will see that as a find and others as a brown veil. Which is it for you?
- **Gliss on jazz drum fills and piano runs:** the first amber smoke appears at the first drum fill.

**Serendipity:**
- **The ring-outs belong to Lazy alone.** After each cadence's D blow the chord rings with no new note. There the dots, both screens and the pebbles dissolve, and the stains are left alone with the one end they bothered to lift on the ff blows (2:16-2:18 and 3:25-3:27). This is the only time in the piece Lazy gets up. The vamp's bass and the first drum fill bring everything back, each on a real note.
- **The terrazzo** in the solo was an accident in a preview, and we kept it.
- **The brushes** come from the audio's high band, placed across the screen by how bright each hit is.
- **A dead end:** halftone's "lines" shape (brushes as hatching) was tried and dropped. It laid a hatch veil over every frame.
