# Gymnopedie (jazz mix) · A · Ink on dusk

**Piece:** Satie, Gymnopédie No. 1, as a jazz-hop band at 104 bpm. It rocks between Gmaj7 and Dmaj7 all its life,
with two statements of the tune, two C-then-D cadences into ring-outs, an interlude, a breakbeat groove with a piano
solo, and a closing song.
**Show:** `shows/gymnopedie-jazz-a-ink-on-dusk.json` (open `/?show=gymnopedie-jazz-a-ink-on-dusk`). Built by
`build-gymnopedie-jazz-a-ink-on-dusk.mjs` in this folder. Preview: `renders/preview/preview-gymnopedie-jazz-a-ink-on-dusk.mp4`.
**Keywords:** dusk light, slow dance, melancholy, smoky late-night.
**Palette:** violet and blue Rothko fields; bone ink and bone flocks; amber lamplight from the current; red only at the
cadences (the brush's slash and enso).

## The idea in one line

Two against one. Wherever the harmony only rocks, there are two of everything: two murmuration dancers and two tall
Rothko lamps, the G chord's on the left and the D chord's on the right, each lit only by its own bars. Wherever the
harmony moves (the band's theme, the solo's climb, the closing song), they become one flock in the middle over one
full-screen room.

## What to judge

- **The two lamps (new: two fields as small copies).** Do the two panels read as a Rothko pair? Each is lit only by
  its own chord's bars, but fields glow slowly. Measured on the preview, the left panel is only about 3 % brighter in
  its G bars, and the right one does not change. So the two chords show mostly as two tints (G deep blue, D plum)
  and two places, not as lamps that blink in turn. Should the lamps pulse harder (the fields' "the playing shows"
  knob)?
- **The handover from two to one at 1:08–1:10.** The lamps go out on the snare pickup, then the band's first bass
  note lights the whole room as the dancers merge.
- **Murmuration on jazz changes.** Same chord, same figure: G always forms on the left, D on the right; in the theme
  the flock draws G, D, Em, C and Am. Between its chords each dancer draws a stepped ribbon. Is it a heartbeat or a
  glitch?
- **Fields + brush (untested).** One bone stroke per phrase over violet bands. Is the ink legible on the fields, or does
  it sit too flat on them?
- **Red as punctuation.** The flocks are bone all through. Red comes only at the doors (a slash on C, an enso on D),
  as a blush in the groove, and at the close. Do the doors land harder for it?
- **A favourites stack on a groove.** Does the groove (3:28–4:12) look as busy as it sounds?

**Serendipity:**
- *The critic's:* the room splits into two tall Rothko lamps wherever the dancers dance, each lit only by its own
  chord's bars. It joins into one room exactly where the dancers join into one flock, and it goes dark on the snare
  pickup so that the band lights it.
- *The composer's:* two murmuration dancers for Gymnopédie's rocking chords. The cadence's D chord is only D+A in the
  transcription, so its figure is a lens, held through each ring-out as an open eye. The amber current over violet
  fields reads as lamplight.

**Knobs moved off our defaults, and why:**
- **fields:** home -1 held (the combo's -0.6 went oxblood on this loud band); chords 0.07 in the room and 0.12 in the
  lamps (higher went teal and green).
- **brush:** size 0.6, stay 3.5, leap 9 (fat worms and a full page at the defaults); the solo's notes have stay 2.2
  and leap 4.
- **murmuration:**
  - strike 1, so every comping chord forms its figure;
  - trail 5 and tight 0.8 on the dancers;
  - hold 1.9 for the dancers (1.2 in the groove), plus a long hold on each held lens;
  - bleed 0.1, raised to 0.3 for the groove dancers, so red stays the doors' colour.
- **current:** hue 32, amber.

**Notes changed by hand:** the brush's copies of the C blows are trimmed to 1.3 s, so C slashes and D draws the enso.
The final hit is lengthened to 2.2 s for the last enso. Soft vamp chords are boosted just enough to form figures.
Kicks and backbeat take the pitch of the bass under them, because drum keys are not pitches. Every murmuration clip
starts on its own first chord, because before it a flock waits as a dark-red knot (red where nothing red was played).

**Known weak spots:**
- The dancers are small, and their stepped ribbon can look like a pulse monitor.
- The hats and ride have no mark of their own.
- The lamps' interlude is very dim.
- There is a half-second dip at 4:12 as the lamps hand over to the room.
- The lamps tint more than they pulse (see "What to judge").
