# Night on Bald Mountain · C "Op-art sabbath"

**Piece:** Mussorgsky, *Night on Bald Mountain* (9:40).
**Concept:** one Bridget Riley stripe field (riley) carries the whole night, and each of its three untested partners gets its own strand: Chernabog's chords glow through its black bands (fifths), the sabbath dance cuts it into staircases of time (time slice), and the climaxes tear it into grey bars (pixel sort). The bell is a lone ring of Riley in the dark. At daybreak the stripes lie down.
**Keywords:** witches' sabbath, Chernabog, church bell, daybreak.
**Palette:** black and bone op-art stripes, lit only by the harmonic web's magenta, red and violet chord shapes; the first colour, ink blue and ochre, comes at dawn.

## What to judge

- **Riley + fifths** (untested). Fifths is drawn with lighten, so the chord polygons only show through Riley's black bands and lenses, like a stencil. Best at 0:16-0:31 and in the Eb glorification at 3:00-3:10 (fill raised: lit violet and bone shapes).
- **Riley + time slice** (untested; time slice's first show). In the sabbath (3:55-5:36) its "now" line follows the dance tune and the stripes break into staircases. At the A major climax (5:51-6:34) it slices in rings, so the field becomes a target cut in time.
- **Riley + pixel sort** (untested). Only the real blows (the A blow, the climax peaks, the last chord) tear the field into grey and white bars. On the 30 hammer strokes (4:59-5:07) a second pixel sort flicks one bar per stroke.
- **The two hushes, new in this pass:** the field is cut away to one slit and rebuilt slit by slit. Does an op-art field read better as a field that is not all there yet?
- **The dawn, new in this pass:** the stripes lie down into a horizon of broad bands.

**Serendipity:**
- (critic) **The hushes take the field away.** In the first pass the darkest sound (2:41) looked as busy as the climaxes. Now, on the Bb hammer's last stroke (2:41.3), the whole field is cut to one vertical slit of night, and each whirl of the build opens two more (2:52.3, 2:56.3). On the Eb climax (3:00.3) the full field slams back. The C# minor hush (3:28.2) is the same gesture turned on its side: one horizontal slot like a stave. The crescendo's whirls open more slots (3:37.1, 3:45.3) until the sabbath (3:55.5) brings back the whole field. Each slit is a separate Riley seen through a mask.
- (critic) **At dawn the stripes lie down.** Dawn's Riley is fitted into a window twice the size of the picture and turned 90 degrees. The night's standing stripes become broad horizontal bands, a horizon, and each chromatic rise swells them into hills. No knob can make the bands this broad (stripe stops at 60).
- (composer) The bell's fading rings lie over the returning dawn bands as a moire (7:43-7:46).
- (composer) Two hard cuts to black, exactly where the orchestra cuts off: the A chord (0:42.45) and the night breaking off (7:20.36).
