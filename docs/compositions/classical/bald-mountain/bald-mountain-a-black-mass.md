# Night on Bald Mountain · A "Black mass"

Show: `shows/bald-mountain-a-black-mass.json` (open `/?show=bald-mountain-a-black-mass`). Build script: `build-bald-mountain-a-black-mass.mjs` next to this file.

- **Piece:** Mussorgsky / Rimsky-Korsakov, *Night on Bald Mountain* (9:40).
- **Concept:** The loved Prelude family (ink, dread, panic), pushed further, on the piece it seems written for. Chernabog's brass soaks into oxblood Rorschach ink, the whirling strings are woven threads, the sabbath is a murmuration of spirits. The church bell stops everything but the flock. Daybreak is a grey Sugimoto sea with one lone light.
- **Keywords:** Chernabog, black mass, shrieking strings, church bell, daybreak.
- **Palette:** oxblood ink and dread red on soot black. Then a grey-gold dawn sea with a white-gold thread and an ember harp band.

## What to judge

1. **The dread family on an orchestra.** Rorschach, squares, threads and casket were loved on the Prelude. Do they hold up across 7 minutes of orchestra with only 56% of the notes transcribed? Does the ink feel like Chernabog, or like the same dim maroon blot from minute to minute?
2. **Murmuration + casket** (untested). At the A major climax (5:51-6:34), the casket cuts and melts the flock's big white chord figures.
3. **Squares + strings + blocks** (untested). Glitch blocks tear the red slabs and the threads on the climax strokes. It is clearest at the 30 hammer strokes (4:59-5:07). Note: the blocks' inverted band shows as teal over red, a photographic negative. Does it belong in an oxblood show?
4. **Murmuration's chord clock as a storyteller.** The figures carry meaning: the bell's lone hand at D (7:20), and the final D major triangle (9:34).
5. **Horizon + the current as a whole ending** (2:15 of quiet). Is the empty sea a brave ending, or too empty?

**Serendipity:**
- **The rose window** (critic): at the Eb climax, "Chernabog glorified" (3:00-3:10), the ink's mirror opens from one fold to eight. For ten seconds the blot becomes an oxblood rose window. It is the only time in the piece the mirror changes, and the threads step back so you can see it.
- **The amen** (critic): the harp's last arpeggio completes D major (9:34). That chord brings the flock back one last time, on the same clock the bell struck. The bell's lone hand at D becomes the D major triangle, with its spoke still on D. It lies on the horizon line and stays alone on black as the sea fades.
- **The bell as a clock** (composer): at the church bell, every stroke (found in the audio; there is no bell track) re-forms the flock, alone on black, into a pale clock hand pointing at D.
- **The hammer strokes foretell the bell** (composer): the 30 hammer strokes flip the flock between clock hands and lenses while blocks tear the slabs.
- **The sunrise is a curve** (composer): horizon's presence climbs from 0 to 1 over two minutes, from a black sea to a lit grey-teal sky.
- **The threads come back as the harp** (composer): an ember band in a strip window, laid on the horizon line.
- **The darkest sound turns the ink to gold** (composer): the low wind hush (2:41) switches the ink to soot and gold, alone on black.
