# Barber: Adagio for Strings · B · Marbled elegy

**Show:** `shows/barber-adagio-b-marbled-elegy.json` (open `/?show=barber-adagio-b-marbled-elegy`). Built by `build-barber-adagio-b-marbled-elegy.mjs`.
**Concept:** every note of the chant is a drop of ink on a still bath, pushing the older ink out into bands. A mirror folds the bath on a line that turns with the bass, so the elegy becomes a symmetrical marbled endpaper. A vine creeps on the low strings. At the climax the marble folds into a rosette and is then torn sideways. After the silence comes a new bath with no mirror.
**Keywords:** mourning, elegy, slow ascent, silence.
**Palette:** oxblood and bone ink, slate in the dark middle, a gold seam at the fold; the hymn is drawn in coloured lines; verdigris and ash on the last chords.

## What to judge

- **Marbling + fold (untested combo).** Does a mirrored marble read as an endpaper, or as a Rorschach blot? Best moments are 5:03-5:37, where the stretto entries fold it into 2, 3, 4 and then 6 mirrors (a bone and oxblood rosette), and 5:37-5:53, the four-way blazon.
- **Marbling + pixel sort (untested combo), climax only (5:28-5:53).** Pixel sort sits above the fold, so its tears cut across the mirror and break the symmetry. Are the tears strong enough? They read as greyish translucent bars.
- **Vine (first show).** Green stems on the low strings, mirrored around the gold seam. Is the lime colour too bright for the palette?
- **Marbling at 19% transcription coverage.** The chant set is padded with notes taken from the audio, and the untranscribed swell at 4:27-4:35 is five chord changes taken from the audio.

**Serendipity:**
- The vine, mirrored by the fold, curls symmetrically around the gold gutter seam like an endpaper ornament. The composer found this in a preview and kept it.
- The hymn is redrawn as rainbow line rings, coloured by pitch round the circle of fifths.
- The rosette folds up with the stretto's entries.
- Moving pixel sort above the fold means the climax literally breaks the mirror, which is why the hymn after the silence has none. Placing it there was the critic's choice.
- On the held chord, the fold's axis is held still (it no longer turns with the bass) and the source side is chosen so that the high climax ink is copied. The result is a heraldic four-way blazon instead of a lone, half-black target.
- Verdigris and ash, the one palette held back until now, appear only on the last chords.
