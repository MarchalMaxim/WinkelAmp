# Liszt, Hungarian Rhapsody No. 2 (orchestral) · C · Optical poem

- **Show:** `liszt-hungarian-2-c-optical-poem`. Open it as `/?show=liszt-hungarian-2-c-optical-poem`.
- **Build:** `node docs/compositions/classical/liszt-hungarian-2/build-liszt-hungarian-2-c-optical-poem.mjs`
- **Concept:** Oskar Fischinger's *An Optical Poem* (1938) was cut to this very rhapsody. This show answers it with Fischinger alone, for 10.5 minutes. The geometry of its copies is the form of the piece:
  - **The slow lassan:** one deep-blue panel.
  - **The fast friska:** the panel breaks into windows, and the tune runs from one window to the next.
  - **The stretta:** the windows fold back into the panel.
- **Keywords:** gypsy fiddle, csárdás dance, cat-and-mouse chase, accelerating frenzy.
- **Palette:** Fischinger's deep blue, with discs, rings and bars in his colours (one per pitch class). Low notes are bars, the middle notes discs and high notes rings. Black is used only at true silences, and around the friska's windows.
- **Knobs:** our defaults everywhere, with three exceptions:
  - scatter 1 in the high whirl and the fireworks, where fast notes make the figure bloom and turn;
  - life 8 on the last blow, so the held chord stays lit and nothing new lands;
  - the clarinet's pp notes are heard at x1.4-1.5 in the slit.

## What to judge

- **One widget as the whole show.** Can Fischinger alone carry 10 minutes when only the placement of its copies changes?
- **New: several copies of one widget layered in one panel.** The copies are full-screen, blended with `lighten`, and shifted and scaled by register:
  - the low strings are sunk and 1.32x;
  - the singing line is in the middle;
  - the violins, clarinet and glitter are raised.

  Each strand keeps its own formation, so a row, a V and a column can stand together in one blue. The price is a slightly pastel palette, because each copy's blue lifts the warm colours toward coral and pink. Judge whether that is acceptable.
- **The friska polyptych.** Each strand gets its own window, and each window dims in its rests (a chase curve). Does the tune read as jumping from window to window?
- **Window shapes as form.** Judge each one:
  - a full-height slit for each cadenza;
  - an altarpiece with a predella;
  - a round window;
  - a six-fold kaleidoscope (every other cell turned 180°);
  - a plunge from window to window.
- **Turned windows.** A window turned -90° makes Fischinger's row stand up as a pitch ladder, low notes at the bottom.

**Serendipity:**
- **Layered copies in one panel.** The cast thought instances of a ground cannot overlay. With `lighten`, three full-screen Fischingers share one blue, each shifted by register. That turned the lassan from thin bands on black back into the single panel the cast asked for, and it lets the stretta fold the friska's windows back together. This is the critic's find.
- **Turned windows as pitch ladders.** This is the composer's find.
- **A kaleidoscope of turned cells.** Also the composer's find.
- **The full-height slit.** Its window is longer than the stage, and the stage cuts it. That way the clarinet dances at full scale.

**Weak spots:**
- The friska windows still draw their dancers at about half size.
- The dip is very small.
- The dissolve is long and sparse.
- In the layered panel the low row can run off the left edge as the figure drifts.
