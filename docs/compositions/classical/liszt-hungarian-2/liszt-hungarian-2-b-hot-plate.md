# Liszt: Hungarian Rhapsody No. 2 (orchestra), B "Hot plate"

Show: `shows/liszt-hungarian-2-b-hot-plate.json` (open `/?show=liszt-hungarian-2-b-hot-plate`).
Built by `build-liszt-hungarian-2-b-hot-plate.mjs` in this folder.

- **Concept:** a new family, heat and liquid. One molten surface (Perlin) carries the whole piece, and its colour and churn tell
  the temperature. Water drops (Leidenfrost) skate on it. In the prestissimo the last water has become steam (Flee). The real
  ff blows go off (Fireworks).
- **Keywords:** accelerating frenzy (the churn follows the measured tempo, so the friska brings the plate to a boil), whirling
  friska, tavern stomping (one big zoomed drop kicked by every stomp), virtuoso fireworks.
- **Palette:** amber molten surface; pearl in the tender song; indigo in the dissolve; amber again for the friska, white-hot
  (pearl) at the last blow. Glass drops with white steam. Fireworks are ember red on the Lento's strokes and a blue flame on every ff tutti.

## What to judge

- **Perlin + Leidenfrost, a first in any show, and as a pair.** Perlin works here as a sea-level instrument. At a low curve
  only the notes raise islands out of the black (the cellos alone, 0:40). At full it is a wall-to-wall surface. Do the
  drops read as ON the surface, or as a separate layer?
- **Leidenfrost as a leitmotif.** The lassan's solo voice is ONE drop (clarinet, G# taps, dance, glitter, the song). The
  friska's violins are a drop that splits into beads. The stomps are one big drop, never split, seen through a 1.7x zoom.
- **The original Fireworks on an orchestra.** These are recoloured to blue on the tuttis: red over amber turned into pink fog.
- **Flee on a dance's frenzy:** the prestissimo's unorganised mass of notes, no picks.
- **Colour as form:** amber, pearl, indigo, amber, then white-hot. Is the temperature story readable without being told?

**Serendipity:** these are the unexpected choices.
- Perlin cast against type: its "how present" knob is used as a sea level, so the black is part of the picture.
- Zoomed windows: a region with w/h above 1 works as a close-up lens on the drops.
- A blue flame on amber. This is the deliberate departure from the cast's "ember-red smoke": blue is amber's complement, and
  a hotter flame burns blue.
- The ONE colour flip of the show is on the last ff blow (10:20.5). The plate turns white-hot and churns at full boil
  through the held chord, which has no new attack, under hanging cyan smoke. The pearl of the tender song returns as
  white-hot metal.
- In the friska the skin crinkles finer as the heat rises: roughness climbs with the pulse.
- Two hard cuts to black (1:32, 4:11) where the orchestra breaks off.

**Notes from the critic's pass.**
- A headless simulation of every Leidenfrost clip found the friska's single 207 s drop clip splitting its water down to
  nothing (around 7:30 and 8:25). An empty plate refills with a drop no note brought: a fake attack.
- To fix it, the friska's water is now poured anew at three turns of the form, each on a real note. The boil is kept low
  until the tremolo. 16 to 22 random seeds ran clean on every Leidenfrost clip.
- The fireworks on 0:26 (velocity 74, not ff) were removed.
