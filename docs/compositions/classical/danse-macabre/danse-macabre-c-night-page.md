# Danse macabre · C "Night page"

**Piece:** Saint-Saëns, *Danse macabre* (7:10). **Show:** `/?show=danse-macabre-c-night-page`
**Concept:** the dance writes its own score on a night page. Every note is chalk on black (Graphic score, paper 0), and
each strand writes on it in its own hand.
**Keywords:** midnight, Death's fiddle, skeletons dancing, the Dies irae, graveyard, cock-crow.
**Palette:** chalk white on night black, red slabs, bone brush strokes, rust melts; bone paper at dawn.

## What to judge

- **Untested combos:** score + squares + brush (the dance), score + squares + stella (the fugato, 2:00-2:30), and
  score + pixel sort (the wounds: every heavy blow melts the page in rust, and the page heals).
- **Stella's first show.** Its frames invert what lies under them. Here they are the midnight border, the bones
  rattling, the fugal voices piling up, an iris on the chromatic ascent, and dark frames on the day page.
- **Two scores at once, in new places:** a small rotated copy and a page turned on its end (see Serendipity).
- **One hand per strand:** squares on the bass, the fat bone brush on Death's fiddle, a thin pen on the waltz tune,
  frames on the bones, rust melts on the blows.

**Serendipity:** (1) *The Dies irae cross* (2:55-3:34): a second score writes only the chorale's top line and bass. It
sits on a page turned on its end, so its staff line stands upright across the night page's own staff line. The result
is a chalk cross, shown for exactly as long as the Dies irae sounds. Its notes fall down the shaft like wax drops, and
the chorale's pixel-sort melts drip beside it. (2) *The waltz pen*: the flute's tune is written in a thin, dry pen in
a squeezed copy under the staff, like the words under a song. (3) *Two leaves* in the woodwind interlude: the main
page goes out and two small, tilted night pages float in the dark, one for the winds and one for the strings, each
framed by its own Stella. (4) *Night to day* at cock-crow: the black page fades and a bone-paper page shows through,
where the rooster's notes are the first black ink. (5) Stella's midnight border comes back inverted (dark on paper)
for the last pizzicato.

**Known weak spots:** theme B tutti (1:26-1:41) is page + one wet stroke on purpose (no audible attacks), but thin for
its loudness. The interlude leaves are dim between attacks. The day page is long and spare (35 s).
