# Danse macabre · A · Midnight on black

**Piece:** Saint-Saëns, *Danse macabre* (7:10). **Show:** `/?show=danse-macabre-a-midnight-on-black`
**Concept:** STYLE's "Bells, a flare and a sinking voice" family, taken to pure black on a piece that has all three.
**Keywords:** midnight bells, Death's fiddle, rattling bones (the xylophone), the ghostly waltz, the dead's round dance, cock-crow.
**Palette:** milk-white keys and slabs, one ink-blue sail, small red bells and squares, silver shards, bone-white ridgelines;
the only colour field is the dawn.

## What to judge

- **Ridges as the ribs: new, not tried in any theme yet.** One full-frame small copy of ridges (all notes) is the dance's
  ground from the first waltz downbeat to the last blow. Its intensity curve tells the story: the dead rise with the waltz,
  sink under Death's solo, heave fully in the tuttis, drop out at the chorale's sudden hush and at the break, and freeze
  on the last blow. Does it fill the black without killing the "on black" idea?
- **Ridges under the fold (5:23-6:00): the strongest new combo here.** The fold mirrors the ridgelines round a seam that
  turns with the bass: a symmetrical ribcage, themes A and B at once. Ridges under the time slice (2:00-2:29) is the same
  idea for the fugato; it reads less well (mostly one cut line).
- **The clock dial, twice.** At midnight, twelve red squares round a clock, one per harp stroke, the twelfth at the top.
  In the frenzy (6:01-6:15) the same twelve places come back, lit one hour per beat (audio beats, 215 bpm; only the 35 of 52
  beats with a detected attack within 60 ms strike, the rest leave a gap in the hand): a red hand with a bone-hot head and
  a cooling tail racing round the dial to dawn. New: handover in twelve small copies, never tried. **The rule on trial:**
  STYLE says handover only on long, slow notes, and rejected it on a fast agitato theme (it flashed). Here each window gets
  one tick every 3 s, so no single slab flashes; does the moving hand escape that verdict? Both dials use handover's red
  (milk 0) on purpose, against the "all handovers milky" default: they stand in for the cast's small red snap bells.
- **Film on Death's fiddle** (retried after its horn-call rejection): the tritone tuning whips the corners; the farewell
  lays them slowly.
- **Mica on the xylophone** (bones), **Ivory on a waltz** (one row per phrase; the solo theme B as a staircase of small
  huddled rows sinking down the screen), **pluck** on the pizzicato, **fireworks-snap** only on the real ff blows.
- Two deliberate pauses: 0:10-0:13 (the hush, nearly black), and 6:17-6:19, where the last blow's red smoke decays
  with no new strike before the oboe's one thin call.

**Serendipity:** ridges cast as a ribcage, the dead's heaving ground, on a show built to stay on black (the critic's
addition); the fold turning those ribs into a symmetrical, turning ribcage in the recapitulation (found in the preview, not
planned); the midnight clock coming back in the frenzy as a hand racing round the dial, one hour per beat; handover, a
melody widget, used as twelve fixed clock positions; and the ribs cut dead at the break and on the last blow.

Known weak spots: the time slice still reads as one cut line, not a canon; ridges has a faint sepia plate behind its
lines; the frenzy is very full (ridges, smoke, dial, sail); the dawn field is a rust rectangle, not pale light; the
bells and the crow were timed from picks and audio analysis, not by ear; the frenzy preview was rendered before the
dial's ticks were filtered to confirmed onsets (17 fewer squares now).
