# Holst: Mars, the Bringer of War · C · "Black film"

**Concept.** Radical near-monochrome: the war as one reel of black-and-silver film. Ridges is the front: every
slice of the music is a ridgeline. Soft passages pack into dim hairlines; the onslaught spreads into bold bright lines.
Len Lye's boiling white scratches carry the col legno ostinato. Ikeda prints the brass as barcodes (the same note
always gives the same code), mostly as the film's optical soundtrack in the left margin. Red glitch blocks tear it all
at the blows.
**Keywords:** col legno strings, mechanised warfare, war machine, dissonant climax.
**Palette:** black and silver film grain: ridgelines, white scratches and barcodes, a few red glitch bars.
**Open:** `/?show=holst-mars-c-black-film` · build: `build-holst-mars-c-black-film.mjs` (this folder).

## What to judge

- **Ridges + Len Lye** in one show: the landscape and the scratched film over seven minutes. Neither has been judged.
- **Ikeda + blocks over ridges**: the armoured return (4:16) and the last blows (6:36), where Ikeda fills the frame
  and blocks tears it.
- **Ikeda used as a region.** It appears as a sideways soundtrack strip, as a second strip that answers the first in
  the canon, and as a band of signals across the sky during the trumpet calls. Do the small copies read, or are they
  too small?
- **Ridges + time slice** (untested) in the canon at 5:04. The slit-scan rebuilds the film from the last bar, so the
  scratches and peaks repeat in steps across the frame.
- Four black-ground widgets layered through clear windows: does the stack stay one family?

**Serendipity:**
- The composer found Ikeda working as an optical soundtrack: a window turned on its side in the empty margin, so the
  barcodes print as horizontal bars. In the canon, a second strip on the right answers it.
- Also the composer's: in the bassless high repeats, the ridges hang upside down from the sky, and they only read once
  the ground was taken away. The general pause wipes the reel, and each of the final blows is its own shot.
- The critic sent the trumpet calls into the sky: the tuba stays low in the side strip, the trumpets print as a band
  of signals over the far landscape.
- The critic cut the canon with a time slice: a voice that answers itself a bar later becomes a picture that answers
  itself a bar later.

**Knobs changed from our defaults, each for the concept:**
- Len Lye: burst 0, except 1.6 on the real blows. fps 8 at the dawn and 20 at the return. dirt 2 in the runout.
- Ikeda: flash 0, except on the final blows (flash 1, slam 1).
- Ridges: sharpness, depth, height and speed set per note at the turns of the form.
- Blocks: settle, jump and amount raised on the climaxes.
- Time slice: far 1.78, one bar of the 5/4 ostinato.
