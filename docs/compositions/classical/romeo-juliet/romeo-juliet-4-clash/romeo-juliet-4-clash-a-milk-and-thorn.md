# Romeo and Juliet, 4. The clash: A "Milk and thorn"

- **Piece:** Tchaikovsky, *Romeo and Juliet* fantasy-overture, the development (pack `romeo-juliet-4-clash`, 2:40).
- **Show:** `shows/romeo-juliet-4-clash-a-milk-and-thorn.json`. Build script: `build-romeo-juliet-4-clash-a-milk-and-thorn.mjs` next to this page.
- **Keywords:** feuding families, the Friar Lawrence chorale, fate, clashing swords.
- **Palette:** milky slabs pierced by red thorns and bone needles; red fate squares over rust, plum and ember fields.
- **The idea:** the feud (thorns, needles, wool) against the Friar's chorale (milky slabs). The Friar comes closer with each entry, from small far-off tiles to a pillar of light at the climax. Fate (small squares) is heard quietly under the horns before it arrives.

## What to judge

- **Handover on a ff brass chorale against Prickle** (the trial for this show), at 1:39-2:02. The trumpets' chorale is a tall, narrow milky column in the middle of the frame, and the thorns stab in at it from both edges. Is the column the right answer, or should the slabs fill the sky?
- **Time slice in ring mode** (not judged before), 1:21-1:36. On Crimp's wool it makes concentric red-and-bone arcs, a vortex that draws in toward the climax. Keep it or drop it?
- **One needle, hammered** (Interlock with ratchet), 2:02-2:16: every F# blow drives the same needle deeper, on black.
- **The hush as a painting**, 0:59-1:10: a plum Rothko field with one bone line and one slab. It is the brightest field of the first half and lights up at the sudden pp, after the dimmer rust of the feud. This inversion is deliberate. Should the picture get brighter when the orchestra drops to pp?
- Crimp used two ways: woolly on the running strings, and pulled straight on held and repeated notes.

**Serendipity:** (1) The pillar: Handover as a small copy stretched into a full-height window (`fit: stretch`), so each trumpet blow is a lancet of milk instead of a card over everything. Prickle's edge-born thorns then aim at it. (2) Time slice `mode` 2 (rings) on wool. I tried rows first, which looked bland. Rings turned the build's strings into a target or vortex that nobody had planned. (3) The cellos' D pedal (0:37-0:47) as a taut low wire that each cello stroke pulls straight again. (4) From the composer: the ratcheted F# needle, and the casket's single sword cut at the feud's return, which shears the picture into a knitted red-and-bone weave.
