# Romeo and Juliet, 1. Friar Laurence: A "Milk and thorn"

- **Piece:** Tchaikovsky, *Romeo and Juliet* fantasy-overture, the introduction (pack `romeo-juliet-1-friar`, 0:00-5:26).
- **Show:** `shows/romeo-juliet-1-friar-a-milk-and-thorn.json` (open as `/?show=romeo-juliet-1-friar-a-milk-and-thorn`).
  Build: `build-romeo-juliet-1-friar-a-milk-and-thorn.mjs`, in this folder.
- **Keywords:** the Friar Laurence chorale, fate, foreboding.
- **Palette:** milk-white slabs and harp hairlines; brick-red string threads (the thorns); a cold grey sea; a dark plum
  field that turns oxblood; the first small red bells.

## What to judge

- **Horizon under snap widgets (new).** The cold sea is the ground of the eerie and hushed passages. Crimp's red
  threads and Handover's slabs are drawn over it. Neither pairing has been shot before. Check whether the red still
  reads on the grey sky: it goes a little pink there, and stays clear on the dark sea.
- **Two grounds that take turns.** The sea belongs to stillness and the field to anguish. They never sit under one
  scene together. Is the handover at the F minor chord (1:33), where the sea burns away, a strong enough gesture?
- **One Crimp clip as the whole string section.** Each note carries its own slack. The threads are straight when the
  strings are calm and fray into wool as they grow anguished. A hush pulls them straight again. Crimp is kept off the
  chorale.
- **Handover on a wind chorale**: milky slabs on long, slow notes. From the second statement the chorale's bass is a
  second slab.
- **Pluck on the harp, and on the pizzicato** in a small copy stretched across the lower half (untested).
- **Snap squares on fate strokes** taken from the audio (low-band onsets), not from the notes.

**Serendipity:** Horizon was not in the cast (critic's addition). The user loved it, and it is tagged still, cool,
strings. The problem it solves is that Crimp cannot hold a long note, so the ppp violins left a black screen. The sea
holds; it makes stillness and anguish two grounds that take turns, and the F minor chord burns one into the other.
Also: one Crimp clip whose per-note slack tells the whole story of the strings (the composer's idea); the pizzicato in
a stretched small copy; the cut at 4:53 that leaves one red square alone.

**Knobs off our defaults, each for a reason:** handover size and per-note intensity (the pp chorale was grey tiles);
crimp slack, fibres, reach and stays per note (the concept); pluck pull 1.6 and ring 1.2 on the harp (the loved
nocturne a5 settings), touch 1 and pull 2 on the pizzicato; squares-snap size 0.7, life 1, shake 0 (calm bells);
fields home fixed, with per-note colour frozen at oxblood in the climax (warming further turned it mustard); horizon
on our defaults (per-note intensity 1.5 on its picks, so the pp notes glint).
