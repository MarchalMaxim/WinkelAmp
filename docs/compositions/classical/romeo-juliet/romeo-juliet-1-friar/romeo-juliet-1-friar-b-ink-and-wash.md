# Romeo and Juliet · 1. Friar Laurence · B "Ink and wash"

**Show:** `romeo-juliet-1-friar-b-ink-and-wash` (open as `/?show=romeo-juliet-1-friar-b-ink-and-wash`)
**Concept:** the story painted in continuous strokes and soaking ink. The Friar's chorale is a glowing harmonic web. The strings lay Klee's warm mosaic. The harp blooms as lace. Fate soaks in as mirrored ink, and at the cut-off it turns to bone.
**Keywords:** Friar Laurence chorale, foreboding, fate.
**Palette:** umber mosaic with lemon, madder and blue glazes; the web in bone, magenta and teal; gold lace; sepia ink with a gold rim; a final bone-pale blot.

## What to judge

- **Klee and Fifths as a chorale pair (untested).** The Friar's web sits in one round window at the centre. When the winds speak, that window turns into a **dark rose window** cut into the wall of strings (the window keeps its own black). When the strings answer, it closes back into the mosaic. Elsewhere the web lies see-through over a dim mosaic. Judge whether the opening and closing window reads as the antiphony between winds and strings (clearest at 3:45-4:04).
- **Mandala on a harp (untested).** The harp's lace blooms inside the same dark window (1:45-2:10, 3:43). The harp has no transcribed track, so its notes come from audio onsets.
- **Rorschach as fate.** It is one thread from the first omen to the end, with sepia ink (`ink` 2). The blots grow from small, quickly drying omens to soaked climax blots. At the cut-off blow, the bone ink (`ink` 5) turns the whole sheet pale at once.
- **Fold as the climax mirror.** It mirrors the whole mosaic and ink into one blot on the ff blows. A faint ghost of it appears earlier, on the eerie high violins.
- **Knobs off our defaults:**
  - fifths `decay` 30 (the web remembers a whole statement) and `line` 1.8;
  - rorschach `ink` 2, then 5 per note after the cut;
  - fold `seam` 0 for the ghost only;
  - klee row width and paint life set per note, to shape the arc.

**Serendipity:**
- **The dark rose window (critic).** The Friar's web is drawn in a round window that keeps its own black. It opens in Klee's wall only while the winds play, so the antiphony becomes a window opening and closing. In a church wall of glaze, it reads as an oculus. The harp's lace blooms in it too.
- **The ghost fold on the high violins (critic).** Fate's mirror appears before its ink.
- **The fold on the climax (composer).** It is outside the cycle family.
- **The bone flip at the cut-off (composer).** One per-note `ink` change turns the whole soaked sheet pale on the last blow.

**For the sibling pieces:**
- The Friar's circle is `{ mode: window, shape: ellipse, x: .5, y: .5, w: .619, h: 1.1, feather: .12, fit: cover }`, with `blend: over` for the dark window and `blend: clear` for the see-through web. Mandala uses the clear one.
- fifths: `decay` 30, `line` 1.8.
- rorschach: `ink` 2.
