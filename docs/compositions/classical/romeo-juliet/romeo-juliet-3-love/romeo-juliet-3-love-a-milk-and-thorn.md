# Romeo and Juliet, 3. Love theme: A "Milk and thorn"

- **Piece:** Tchaikovsky, *Romeo and Juliet* fantasy-overture, the love-theme pack (221 s): the night pedal, the love theme on English horn and violas, the muted sighs, the theme climbing in the winds, the full-string climax, the codetta and the balcony night.
- **Concept:** the love theme rises as milk-white Ivory keys, one short row per phrase. The rows grow from a small, low first statement to full-frame stomps at the climax. The sighs are velvet, and the bass strokes are a heartbeat in a violet field.
- **Keywords:** love theme, sighing strings, balcony night, star-crossed lovers.
- **Palette:** milk-white keys, slate velvet (oxblood at the climax), a dim violet field, a lot of black.
- **Show:** `shows/romeo-juliet-3-love-a-milk-and-thorn.json`. **Build:** `build-romeo-juliet-3-love-a-milk-and-thorn.mjs` (run with tsx).

## What to judge

- **Nap's first show (never judged).** Its job is the muted strings' rocking chords. Each chord strokes a band of velvet, and the same chord again strokes it out. Watch 0:41-1:12: is it a sigh, or only cloth?
- **Nap seen close up (new).** A 2x magnifying window (`fit: cover`) turns a thin column into a floor-to-ceiling curtain. A side effect: inside a window, the velvet now shows over the violet field.
- **Ivory on an orchestral love theme (first time).** Judge:
  - whether the rows' growth (small and low, then each modulation higher and wider, then full frame) reads as the theme gaining confidence;
  - its own ageing to tan, brown and grey before the keys vanish. That is the widget's dissolve, not a colour cue.
- **Upside-down Ivory (new).** The horns' countermelody hangs from the top of the frame in a window turned 180 degrees. Romeo's theme stands up from the ground, and the answer hangs from the balcony.
- **The cloth as identity.** Slate Nap is the quiet strings: the sighs, the swells and the night's falling figure. Oxblood Nap is the full strings at the climb and the climax. The curtain goes almost black for a moment at 2:40, when the same chords stroke their bands out (the sigh, by design).
- **Pluck on the harp.** Only two short arpeggios (0:39, 3:06). The threads are thin; is that enough?

**Serendipity:**
- Nap is seen through a magnifying window (the critic's change). The sighs span only C3-B4, so Nap stroked a thin column; at 2x the velvet becomes a curtain, and it can lie over the field.
- The horns' countermelody is Ivory hung upside down from the balcony (the composer's choice).
- The violet-to-red crossfade is made of heartbeats: each bass stroke steps the field back (the composer's choice).
- The balcony night's field listens to the C#2 heartbeat again, so the piece ends on the pulse it began with.

**Still open:** the climb has thin rows (2-3 keys) and a near-empty dip at 1:47-1:51 (a real -40 dB breath). The upside-down horn rows on repeated Eb/D/F hang like an even comb. The harp's threads are modest.
