# Romeo and Juliet, 2. The feud · A "Milk and thorn"

**Piece:** Tchaikovsky, *Romeo and Juliet* fantasy-overture: the feud (Allegro giusto, B minor, 0:00-1:58), from the
first syncopated accents to the hushed transition before the love theme.
**Concept:** the two houses thrust at each other as huge red thorns from alternating edges. The sword clashes are pairs
of crossing needles. The running strings are woolly red ribbons, over a rust field that heats in the tutti.
**Keywords:** feuding families, clashing swords, syncopated strings.
**Palette:** red thorns and bone needles on black, over an oxblood field; gold mirror seams; a cold plum night at the end.

## What to judge

- **Prickle** (its first show) on the syncopated accents only, at most about two a second. Does one thorn per accent
  read as the feud's off-beat stab, from the left and then the right?
- **Interlock on the cymbal clashes** (taken from the audio). Does the mesh of needles visibly thicken volley by volley?
- **Crimp** on the running strings: ribbons at each note's pitch.
- **Fold as the form's mirror.** Nobody has judged it yet. It appears three times: (1) the fugal chase, where the strings
  imitate each other, so the picture mirrors itself; (2) **the build: a kaleidoscope that gains a mirror on each
  semitone the bass climbs**; (3) the hush, where the mirrors unwind as the winds' figure falls.
- The **casket** used as two sword strokes only (a shear at 1:13.8, a melt at 1:36.0), and **pixelsort** with no paint
  of its own, tearing what is on screen on the ff blows. Both pairings are untested.

**Serendipity:** fold, which is not in the cast, became the show's spine. In the chase the ribbons fold into chevrons,
and the thorns arrive mirrored from both edges at once: the two houses, "both alike in dignity", as mirror images (the
composer's find). In the critic's pass the build had sat empty for 10 s of crescendo. Now each chord there carries a
fold count, 1 to 6, stepping up with the bass (F, F#, G, Ab, A, then the last push). The thorns, needles and piled
ribbons close into stars, a pentagon and finally a hexagonal rose window, and the clip ends exactly on the first sword
clash (1:01.02): the window shatters into the flat fight. In the hush the mirror comes back on the clarinet and bassoon
and unwinds (3, 2, 1 folds) as their figure falls.
