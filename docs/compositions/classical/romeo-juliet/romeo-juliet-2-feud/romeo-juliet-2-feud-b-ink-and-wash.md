# Romeo and Juliet, 2. The feud: B "Ink and wash"

Tchaikovsky's overture-fantasy, the feud (Allegro giusto, B minor, 0:00-1:58). Show: `/?show=romeo-juliet-2-feud-b-ink-and-wash`

- **Keywords:** feuding families, clashing swords, syncopated strings.
- **Palette:** graphite pens with red scratches, bone Kline bars (blood red from the loudest crash), over Klee's ochre mosaic.
- **Family:** scribble (the strings), kline (the clashes), klee (all notes, the ground).

## What to judge

- **Kline on cymbal clashes, in a full-frame see-through window** (untested). The harder clashes strike bars and the softer
  ones lay short brush marks, so each volley comes out as a graded, broken line. Should every clash be a bar instead?
- **Bone turning to blood.** A second Kline clip (colour 1) takes over on the loudest crash (1:13.8). From there every
  bar is red. Is that too much red, or is it the peak?
- **Scribble on fast orchestral strings** (untested). Do the nervous pens and red scratches read as the syncopated feud?
- **The three staves** (new). In the fugal chase, one scribble per string section in its own band, as a score of three
  voices. Does a widget split by place read as polyphony?
- **Klee as a ground that changes its density.** It is a full carpet in the tutti, bare rows in the chase and the hush.
  Does the mosaic help the picture, or is it mush under the bars?

**Serendipity:**

- **The three staves** (critic). One widget in three places, one per fugal voice. Each pen races only when its own section runs.
- **Blood from the loudest crash** (composer). A second Kline clip turns every later bar red.
- **The scar** (composer). The last blow's bars stop soaking and fade only through the hush, crossed by one dry beam on the held A.
- **The breath as a black page** (critic). The first mosaic fades out and the pens' lines clear, so the crash lands on almost nothing.

**Knobs (beyond our defaults):**

- **Kline:** thresh 0.55, so more clashes strike bars; colour 1 on the blood clip.
- **Staves:** pens 1, speed 0.8 (1.9 per note in a run), weight 2 and stay 12, so each voice reads as one line of handwriting.
- **Klee:** curves on how present.
- **Scribble (full-frame pens):** per-note stay (rising through the build; 1.5 in the breath), and loops and speed per note for the hush.
