# Romeo and Juliet, 5. Return and catastrophe: A "Milk and thorn"

- **Piece:** Tchaikovsky, *Romeo and Juliet* fantasy-overture, the return pack (220.5 s). It runs through the sighs in D major, the love theme ecstatic on the whole string body, the break, the feud's return, the catastrophe, the freeze on G, one blow and the dying timpani roll.
- **Concept:** the love theme comes back as milk-white Ivory rows at full height, one per rising phrase. Each phrase's falling answer is an oxblood velvet stroke (Nap), so the opening's sighs return as the theme's answer. Fate's six low knocks press the last row into the ground key by key. Then the feud returns as thorns, wool and swords, and in the catastrophe the swords are meant to build a cage that the violins' collapse shatters (see "What to judge" for how far that reads).
- **Keywords:** love theme, feuding families, fate, tragic ending.
- **Palette:** milk-white keys and oxblood velvet over a rose field, then red thorns, red-and-bone swords and red bells on near-black. A cold plum freeze, and the rose again at the very end.
- **Show:** `shows/romeo-juliet-5-return-a-milk-and-thorn.json`. **Build:** `build-romeo-juliet-5-return-a-milk-and-thorn.mjs` (run with tsx). **Preview:** `renders/preview/preview-romeo-juliet-5-return-a-milk-and-thorn.mp4`.

## What to judge

- **Ivory on an ff orchestral theme, struck hard** (strike 1, crater 0.22, ripple 3). There is one row per rising phrase, and each row ends on the phrase's peak with a stomp. Do the full-height rows read as ecstasy, or as a bar chart?
- **The duet (new, 1:09-1:18).** The violas sing the great chromatic climb an octave below the violins. Their row hangs from the ceiling: Ivory in a window turned 180 degrees. At 1:14.8 both rows stomp together and lock into one block of ivory.
- **Fate pressing the keys (1:40-1:54).** After the theme breaks off, the last row stands in the silence. Each of the six low knocks sends one key down, tallest first, with a small red square.
- **Nap as the theme's answer.** The same oxblood cloth carries the opening sighs, the love theme's falling answers and the broken fragments.
- **The catastrophe on near-black (2:19-3:22, a third of the show), after the critic's pass.** The aim was for the thorns to pile up and for the swords to build a cage over 50 s and shatter it.
  - **In the preview:** the ground is now near black, so the red reads stark instead of murky. Taut wires fill the strings' syncopated chords. But most frames still carry one to three marks. The older needles sink to dark-red wedges, not a mesh, and the shatter at 3:18.8 is hard to tell from the end of the collapse.
  - **To judge:** starker, yes; full, not yet. This is the open question of the show.
- **Interlock in a 2x-wide window (new, a knob idea on trial).** The swords' pitches sit in a narrow band. Stretching the widget's keyboard to twice the picture's width spreads them from left to right instead of one spot right of centre. The needles also come out twice as broad.

**Serendipity:**
- **The upside-down violas' row (the composer's).** It meets the violins' row like interlocking needles, so the love duet is drawn in the same geometry the feud's swords use later.
- **The exact knocks (the composer's).** Fate's knocks turned out to be 1.8 s apart, so the row goes down key by key, exactly on the beat.
- **The swords' cage (the critic's).** A knob pushed over time tells the story: how long the mesh stays climbs from 1.6 to 4 s across the catastrophe, and one real clash sets it back to 0.35 to shatter it.
- **Interlock in a window twice as wide as the picture (the critic's).** It zooms the keyboard onto the band where the swords actually sound.

**Knobs off our defaults, each for a reason:**
- **Ivory:** strike 1, crater 0.22 (0.3 on the last row), ripple 3.
- **Interlock:** width 0.7-1.3, which reads as 1.4-2.6 in the 2x window; bite 1.8 on the two loudest volleys; stays per clash.
- **Prickle:** broad 1.5 on the ff blows, goneBy 2.4 in the catastrophe.
- **Squares-snap:** size 0.7, life 1, blur 0.6, shake 0; per note, size by stroke and shake 1.6 on the four heaviest.
- **Nap:** rest 5; 9 on the answers, 6 on the broken theme.
- **Crimp:** slack, fibres, reach and stays per note.
- **Fields:** home, chords, back, n and dyn per clip.

**Still weak:** the catastrophe is better and starker, but it is still sparse between volleys (about 2:42-2:55). Interlock's older needles darken to a deep red, so the cage reads as a few standing wedges rather than a dense mesh. The next step would be to let the swords also hear the ff blows' chords, with care, because STYLE.md rejects a crowded coda.
