Winter is a show about the mapping between instrument and picture. YourMT3+ splits the recording into instrument tracks: violins, violas, cellos, and a little keyboard ("piano"), which is probably the harpsichord continuo. Each layer listens to one track and gets only that track's notes and pulse. When an instrument is silent, its widget goes quiet too.

The mapping, in plain words:
- violins → woven threads (strings). The silky, singing top line: every violin note is a thread in the weave.
- violas → the current. A thin golden thread in dark water, the quiet middle voice. It is a fluid, so it keeps drifting faintly when the violas rest. It brightens and pours dye when they play.
- cellos → dread squares. Heavy, dark-red slabs, one per cello note. Under `listen: cello` every note is a cello note, so the split is raised to 72 (the cellos go up to F3–A♭3) and the squares are made smaller (size 0.55).
- keys, the continuo → halftone. Fine crisp dot screens that ripple out on each chord (a finer screen, shorter waves and fewer stripes than the defaults, so it stays a small, pointed voice).

The showcase is the first 1:38 (0–100 s, one section, one stack, so nothing cuts in the middle):
- 1.1 s: the cellos start alone on a low F, and the squares land.
- 2.1 s: the violas join on the same repeated F, an octave up, and the current wakes.
- 4.8 s: the violins pile in one after another, and the threads appear and thicken.
- 42–44 s, 49–53 s and 57–66 s: the violas and cellos drop out under the solo violin's runs. The squares fade, and only the weave is left.
- 66 s: the tutti comes back, and so do the squares and the current.
- 88.3 s: the keyboard enters with repeated chords, and the halftone dots ripple under the threads.

The rest of the piece keeps the same idea, with simpler sections:
- The solo against the tutti (100 s): the murmuration listens to the violas. The weave curls into arcs.
- The storm (146 s): Kandinsky on all notes, with icy blue violin threads.
- The Largo (211 s): the solo line as icy woven rings over a still sea (horizon). The halftone listens to the "viola" track, which here is mostly the pizzicato accompaniment: the rain.
- The finale opens on the ice (352 s): Ikeda's white-on-black data driven by the violin alone, with the cello squares.
- The ice cracks (395.5 s): Rorschach ink on the cellos, with the violin weave.
- A hush (497 s): the sea and the viola current.
- The winds at war (519 s): Kandinsky, the cello squares, the violin weave, and the casket cutting it all to the end.

Where YourMT3+'s labels look off:
- In the Largo, the "viola" track (around 213–230 s, a dense run at MIDI 50–58) sounds like the pizzicato violins.
- The single "guitar and harp" burst (75.9–78 s) is one repeated F-minor triad (C3, F3, A♭3), probably strings or keyboard.
- In the opening, the violas and cellos play the same rhythm an octave apart, so their two widgets pulse together there.
