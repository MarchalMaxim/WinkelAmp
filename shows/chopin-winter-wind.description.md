Winter Wind is a storm of thin, silvery right-hand runs over a heavy left hand that marches. The runs are wind and ice: fast, pale, blue and white. The march is dark and weighty: red slabs, ink and black bars. The picture changes with the form, and the storm gets its own picture back when it returns.

The Lento (from 5 s) is four quiet bars on one note, E. First the right hand plays alone: a grey, still sea (horizon) with a single thread of the current. Then the theme is harmonised (from 12.8 s) and a faint harmonic web strings the chords over the same sea. The storm breaks at 21.7 s, on a low A and a high F struck together, as a hard cut. Icy woven threads (strings, ink blue and ice) are the runs. Dread squares are the left hand's march; their split is raised to 61 so that the march's A3–C4 chords make slabs, not only the deepest bass. Skittish shapes in their ice palette are the gusts.

When the second phrase turns towards C major (39.4 s), Kandinsky takes over under the same ice gusts. The theme moves to E minor (61.7 s), the dominant, with heavy low Es and the first loud chords. That part is Ikeda's black-and-white data with the red march slabs on it, and the casket cuts the chords up to the cadence at 78.5 s. A softer bar in C (79.5 s) is a breath: only the nebula's glowing grains. It carries on into the far keys (88 s: B minor, D minor, B-flat, F minor) over Delaunay's discs, whose colours follow every modulation.

At 104 s the theme sinks into the bass in B-flat minor: indigo Rorschach ink with the squares. A lighter stretch through A-flat, B and E major (112 s, no big chords) is the halftone's dot screens and the current. Then comes an outburst in E-flat minor, a tritone from home (121 s, the loudest bass since the start): the indigo ink and the squares come back, and the casket cuts them. The circle-of-fifths sequence (128 s: G, C, F, B, E, A, D…) is a LeWitt wall drawing, a rule carried out step by step, with the harmonic web over it. The peaks of the build-up (146 s: high F and B over low E and B) rise as Ridges, smeared by the pixel sort.

The hush before the return (154 s) is the only quiet moment of the Allegro: a soft, broken diminished figure. It gets the Lento's sea and single thread back. The runs creep upward again (158 s) as Riley's cold cyan stripes, which the bass bends into a lens as it builds. The storm returns with the same low A and high F (164.4 s) and the same picture as at 21.7 s. The second phrase turns again (181.8 s) as marbling in indigo and gold. The keys whirl (190 s) as Kusama's dots, whose colour pair flips with the harmony. The runs high in the treble (198 s) are the murmuration over deep blue colour fields.

The coda (207 s) hammers low As under the runs: oxblood ink, the squares, and glitch blocks tearing them on every attack. The chromatic descent in octaves (214.5 s) is smeared downward by the pixel sort. The fortissimo A minor chords (217.2–221.4 s) are Kline's bone-white beams, one per chord, with the casket on top. The last ascending scale (222.1 s, up to the high A at 224.3 s) is written out on a graphic score, which holds while the last chord rings. From 231 s the recording goes on into another piece (A-flat major, repeated E-flat octaves), played quietly under Miró's pale sheet.
