# Round 6: after the visual-music pioneers (2026-10-31)

Fourteen new widgets, each after an artist or film-maker who painted or animated music. They sort to the top of the lab
(`npm run dev`, then http://localhost:5173/lab/index.html, and `?song=b5` for Beethoven). All are **not judged yet**. Each
has 5 knobs, every knob with a plain sentence and a small picture at both ends. Videos: `lab/showcase/31-…` to `44-…`,
and `00-showreel-round6.mp4` (6 s of each).

| widget | what it is after | what to look for |
|---|---|---|
| Fischinger | Optical Poem: shapes dancing in formation | Does the same chord really give the same figure? Do the hops land on the beat? |
| McLaren | Synchromy: you see the soundtrack | Deep notes thick rungs, high notes a fine comb; do the panels split in a way that feels musical? |
| Len Lye | scratched film | Lively or just noisy? The loud-chord stars are new (not lightning, not glass). |
| Whitney | dot rosettes from intervals | A fifth = 2 arms, a fourth 3, a third 4 or 5: can you see the harmony? |
| Klee | polyphony as a mosaic | Can you follow the voices along their rows? Do the mixed colours read? |
| Miró | Constellations: one line, stars and blobs | Does the line follow the melody? Does the sky stay readable in the climax? |
| Delaunay | simultaneous colour discs | Lit segments at each note's clock position, opposite colours beside them. Too busy? |
| Albers | Homage to the Square | Very calm: colours change only with the chord. Warm in Rachmaninoff, cool in Beethoven. |
| LeWitt | wall drawings by rule | The lines pile up; a new section starts a fresh wall with the next rule, written in pencil. |
| Ridges | the Unknown Pleasures cover | Peaks by pitch and loudness, rolling away. Clear, but maybe too literal a quote? |
| Matisse | cut-outs | Pieces swap in and out; chords move them. Does it feel like the music or just pretty? |
| Kusama | dots and infinity nets | Bold full-screen colour; the colours flip with the harmony. Too loud for the dread? |
| Ikeda | black-and-white data | Barcodes per note on their note-name rows. Strobing on attacks: too much? |
| Horizon | Sugimoto's seascapes | Almost still by design, a background for other widgets. Try it under Fischinger or Miró. |

## The contact sheets
One tile per widget, widget alone, each about 2 s after the time in its name: `prelude-130s` the casket (the climax),
`b5-30s` Beethoven's agitato. (Reduced to 256 colours to keep them small; the lab shows the real colours.)

## Known weaknesses
- Most of the 14 paint their own ground (blue, umber, paper, plaster, flat colour), so in a mix they hide the widgets drawn
  before them. Horizon puts itself first on purpose, as a background.
- **Len Lye** is sparse in Beethoven's quiet stretches; **Horizon** barely moves anywhere, on purpose.
- **McLaren** stacks horizontal bars in its panels (as in Synchromy), not upright stripes; busy music, not chord size, splits the panels.
