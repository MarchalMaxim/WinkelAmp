Stills can't show what these are about, which is how they move.

**Nobody has watched any of these move at real speed with sound.** Everything below was judged from frames and measurements. Most of them also changed after their last critic, and no new critic has looked since, so "last critic" below means the version before the final polish.

## Which slider to try first

Every one of them also has "how present": far left it goes faint and only the piece's hardest attacks get through. (Royal's figure stays firmer than the rest at far left. The casket's far left is a faint strike, and far fewer of them.)

- **Clumsy**: clumsiness, tumbling over each other to keep up, and every attack ruins the order again. Try "how hard an attack knocks them", on a slow piece, where they settle between attacks.
- **Brass gust**: trumpets, gusty, full of power. Try "how much force".
- **Duet**: two forms in two colours that attract and repel. Try "how hard a clash throws them apart", then "how strongly they want each other".
- **Vine**: spreads and withers. Try "how fast it withers"; give it 15 seconds or more to grow first.
- **Royal**: royal, elegant, dismissive. Try "how far it turns away".
- **Comfort**: comfortable, relaxing, slow. Try "how slowly it breathes".
- **Honey**: droopy, viscous, slow. Try "how thick it is".
- **Lazy**: lazy, lethargic, green. Try "how lazy it is".

## What a critic still found short

Each line gives the last critic's verdict, then what is still short after the polish that followed it.

- **Clumsy** (passed): "tumbling over each other" is the weakest phrase. A shape coming down makes its neighbour flinch, but they overlap rather than really stack or climb. On hard Winter Wind chords (about 0:22) the crowd is briefly off a tilting floor and floats. Most of the crowd takes 1.4 to 2.8 s to settle, longer than the 1 to 1.5 s the critic wanted. At rest a still can look like toy blocks, and the top half is bare. "How many" at its far left is jumpy.
- **Brass gust** (failed narrowly, on the climax turning into even straw): the climax now breathes, with dark lulls and only accents crossing the screen, but it never hits as hard as a single blast on a dark ground. If you jump into a dense run, the first moment can still cross strands. Knights around 1:43 stays busy and hatched. Resting air is sparse commas more than a current, and strand tails can show stair-steps.
- **Duet** (failed narrowly, on turning into a bird or a plane in the quiet): about 1 or 2 of 16 quiet stills can still suggest a thing, like a fish, a leaf or a fin. They change within seconds. The orange's outline can read as a human profile, and the ghost outline at the top of its `sheet.png` frame does. The sharp form is chunkier, so less thin and cold than it was. A small overlap during a lunge can show a pale violet patch with a hard edge. In dense music you feel agitation more than separate hits, except on the loudest accents.
- **Vine** (passed; only one critic round): the most literal of the eight, since green and tendrils read as plant. The first thing to try if you think so is the colour. Dense pieces reach an even tangle at about 30 s, before withering thins it at about 70 s. An attack moves the tips and buds, and the settled network never stirs, so you read sync by watching tips. The critic's other point, a weak opening, is fixed: loud first chords now shoot much further.
- **Royal** (failed closely, on the gold arc beside the tip and the court drowning the figure): both are fixed. Between flicks in thin passages being ignored reads as aloof rather than actively dismissive. Attacks show less now that the court is quieter, and in dense runs only the top attacks flash. The tip goes very thin when cocked. Other robes were not looked at.
- **Comfort** (failed narrowly, on melody notes you could not see): nearly all isolated notes now show, though two low Nocturne notes and one in Liebesleid still don't. A melody note is a gentle brightening, on purpose. It won't catch your eye unless you raise "how far a note presses". The room is mid-bright rather than a dim evening. A few moments can still be named: a round terracotta field in the Nocturne near 1:00, a peach-like orb in the Rachmaninoff concerto at 5:00.
- **Honey** (failed closely, on a springy attack, plucked threads and threads from the top edge): all three are gone. It is still opaque, and flat amber reads closer to Arp than to honey. Sync is weakest in soft fast passages (Prelude about 1:30). A big body can stay pale through a long loud passage. Beads can look like dots on a wire, a snapped tail is briefly a tadpole, and late passages can turn into separate potato shapes. Not run in a stack, and not on Chopin.
- **Lazy** (failed, as lettuce or a bush on a hill; since reworked, and this is the biggest change no critic has seen): now soft stains on a green ground. The blur is a new risk: an out-of-focus photo of foliage, or camouflage, with clover-like lobes in Winter Wind at 0:30. Rings can still form. In busy music its jolts land about once a second on whichever note comes next, not on accents, so they could read as a slow pulse. The cost of its blur on a real graphics card is not measured.
- **The casket** (passed): of the Prelude's 51 hardest chords only 11 strike, because a lesser chord just before can use up the wait, and gaps of 3.4 to 4.3 s can feel like a slow pulse more than picked moments. "Which attacks strike" opens that up. I left the default, since changing it could change the shows that use the old casket without setting it. The drips are its most literal part: over sparse pictures they look like pills. Alone it is only a line and bars over black. It has 7 sliders, one over the rule, because of "keep melting between"; it can go once you've settled the shows.

## The casket

You said: a one-time applied widget, applied once on some attacks instead of continuously. Now it does that: on the hardest
attacks of what it hears, with room between them, one melt, its drips and one cut land and finish. Between strikes it leaves the picture
alone. If an attack stands alone (2 s or more after the one before), it always lands, so sparse parts work too. Because of this, a widget that repaints itself every frame, like Rorschach, now
shows the struck picture melting away over the live one.

Sliders: "which attacks strike" now sets how far apart strikes are (left: every few seconds; right: only the very hardest, far apart).
New: "how present", and "keep melting between", which goes from the new way on the left to the old casket on the right. "Melt" at far left now really holds still (the struck picture used to freeze over the live one for a second; in a 12 s run at far left that picture is no longer drawn at all).

**What the existing shows now hold:**

- `beethoven5` (15 casket layers), `chopin-winter-wind` (3), `rach-prelude-recording` (1): "keep melting between" is set to 1, the old casket.
- `vivaldi-winter` (1 layer, from your own edit, cut at 118, no jolt): "keep melting between" is added at 1 on that layer, nothing else changed, so your own show looks as it did.
- `vivaldi-summer` (1 layer, "The last blows"): not set, so **it uses the new one-time casket**. If you want it as it was, set "keep melting between" to the far right on that layer.
- `chopin-nocturne-48-1` (2 layers, your own show, saved while the casket was still the old one): "keep melting between" is added at 1 on both, nothing else changed, so your own show looks as it did. For the new one-time casket, move it to the far left on those layers.
- The three lab combinations with the casket (brush and threads, murmuration, Kandinsky and pixel sort) set "keep melting between" to 1 when you press "try together", because what they describe is the continuous melt.
